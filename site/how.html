Method

How it works

Three public sources, joined by ticker. No account, no wallet connection needed to read anything.

  1. Read the filing

    FERRET pulls 13F reports (what a fund holds) and Form 4 reports (what an insider traded) from SEC EDGAR.

  2. Find the token

    Each stock is looked up in Robinhood's official stock token registry. If a token exists, the row gets its address.

  3. Show the match

    You see the filing and the token side by side, with a link to the SEC document and to the token on the explorer.

How fresh is it

  • Fund booksFunds file a 13F up to 45 days after the quarter ends. A book shows what the fund held then, not today.
  • Insider tradesForm 4 is due within two business days of the trade. The feed keeps trades over $25K.
  • Wallet balancesRead live from Robinhood Chain when you press Check.
  • RefreshThe filing data is rebuilt once a day.

Questions

Do I need $FERRET to use the site?

No. Everything on it is free. The token gives a vote on which fund is added next.

Where does the data come from?

SEC EDGAR for 13F and Form 4 filings, Robinhood's stock token registry for token addresses, and Robinhood Chain for balances and prices.

Why are some holdings marked "no token"?

That stock has no stock token on Robinhood Chain yet. It stays in the table so the book is complete.

Who can buy stock tokens?

Stock tokens are issued by Robinhood Assets (Jersey) Limited. They are not available to US persons or in several other places, including the UK and Canada.

Is this investment advice?

No. FERRET shows what public filings say. Copying a fund or an insider can lose money, and nothing here tells you to buy or sell.
